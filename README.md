I live where people, systems, and information meet. Before teaching, I spent about eight years in industry. After roughly a year of engineering internships, including at Suncor and Facebook, I dropped out of Waterloo Engineering to build a startup. We sold what we built; somehow, I made it back and graduated without losing time. That winding path led to seven years at Palantir.

At Palantir I built and deployed data and machine-learning systems, led fast-moving teams, worked with product teams, and learned from very smart end users to help organizations change how they operated.

At first, the fun was building machines and teaching them to learn from data. Over time, the harder and more interesting part became people: leading teams, learning with end users, and helping organizations change. Teaching felt like a natural next step. Humans require a few more inputs, so now I guide people to learn by doing and from one another, so they can eventually teach machines themselves.

I care about making useful knowledge understandable, reusable, and freely available. Along the way I also ended up with two U.S. patents and a University of Waterloo teaching award. The details are in LinkedIn and my CV.
