I live where people, systems, and information meet. Before teaching, I spent about eight years in industry: roughly a year of engineering internships, including at Suncor and Facebook, followed by seven years at Palantir. Somewhere in between, I dropped out of Waterloo Engineering to build a startup, sold what we built, came back, and somehow graduated without losing time.

At Palantir, I built and deployed data and machine-learning systems, led fast-moving implementation teams, worked with product teams, and learned from very smart end users. The interesting problem was rarely just the model; it was implementing large organizational change through systems.

At first, the fun was building machines and teaching them to learn. Over time, people became the more interesting system. Teaching felt like a natural next step: I guide people to learn by doing and from one another, so they can build and teach machines themselves — and then I get to learn from them.

I care about making useful knowledge understandable, reusable, and freely available. Along the way I also ended up with two U.S. patents and a University of Waterloo teaching award. The details are in LinkedIn and my CV.
